# Notes on slow reading

I started timing my reading last spring. Not to read faster, but to stop guessing how long an article would take before I opened it. A long read on a train needs a different kind of attention than a short note between meetings, and I kept picking the wrong one.

## What I measured

For six weeks I wrote down the start and end time of everything longer than a page. Newspapers, essays, chapters, the odd technical manual. Then I counted the words and divided. My average came out at a little over 210 words a minute for essays and close to 160 for anything with tables or numbers. That gap surprised me more than the averages did.

The slow pieces were not harder to understand. They just asked me to look back more often. A table makes you check a row twice. A paragraph full of dates makes you stop and place each one on a timeline. Prose that flows lets you move forward without turning around.

## Why the number matters

Once I had a rough pace, I could plan. A twelve minute essay fits on the morning bus. A forty minute report does not, so it waits for a quiet hour on Friday. This sounds trivial, and it is, but it changed how often I finished things. Before, I would open a long article, read a third of it, get interrupted and never come back. Now I open it when I have the time it needs.

It also made me more honest about my notes. Some of my own project pages had grown to the length of a short story. Nobody, including me, was going to read them before a meeting. Seeing "18 min read" at the bottom of a page I meant as a summary was a clear sign to cut.

## Things that do not count

A word count that includes everything is misleading. My notes have link targets, embedded images and small code snippets that I never read aloud in my head. A page with thirty links to other notes looks long if you count every path, but it reads in two minutes.

## Selections

Sometimes I only want to know how long one section takes. When I prepare a talk, I select the part I plan to read out and check its time. Spoken pace is slower than silent reading, roughly 130 to 150 words a minute for me, so I set a slower pace in the settings while I rehearse and switch back afterwards.

## Habits that stuck

Three things lasted beyond the experiment.

1. I check the length before I start, and I pick reading that fits the time I have.
2. I keep my own summaries under five minutes. If a summary takes longer, it is not a summary.
3. I write the reading time into the properties of long notes, so my weekly review table can sort them from short to long.

None of this made me a faster reader. It made me a reader who finishes more of what he starts, which was the point all along.

## Open questions

I still do not know how to estimate pages that mix languages. My notes from a trip to Japan have English paragraphs next to Japanese place names and menu items. Counting the Japanese text by words makes no sense, because it has no spaces between words. Counting characters at a separate pace seems closer to how long it actually takes me.

I also wonder whether reading time should change with the time of day. I read slower at night, but I am not going to keep two settings for that.
